ENERGY STORIES:

Saudi Arabia has suspended operations on a major oil pipeline following a drone strike attributed to Iraq, marking another escalation in regional tensions. The attack, which targeted the East-West pipeline—a crucial route for crude exports—originated from a province near the Iran border, according to Iraqi officials. The incident has raised concerns about the stability of global oil supplies, as the pipeline accounts for a significant portion of Saudi output.

The Saudi government has opted not to retaliate, citing diplomatic pressure from Iraq. Both nations are now engaged in an investigation into the attack, which reportedly caused damage and injuries. The pipeline, operated by state-owned Aramco, has been temporarily halted as security teams assess the situation. Satellite images show signs of damage, with smoke and scorched ground reported near the site.

The attack comes amid a broader conflict involving Iran-backed Houthi rebels in Yemen, who have expanded their control along the Red Sea coast. This has intensified pressure on oil shipping routes, contributing to a surge in energy prices. Analysts suggest the strike may have been orchestrated by an Iranian-aligned group, aiming to weaken Saudi influence in the region.

Global oil prices have climbed above $100 per barrel, marking the highest level since July. The situation has exacerbated inflation fears and increased borrowing costs for governments. The attack highlights the growing risks to energy infrastructure and the complex web of alliances and conflicts in the Middle East.

The incident underscores the fragility of energy security in the region, with tensions continuing to reshape global markets and geopolitical strategies.
