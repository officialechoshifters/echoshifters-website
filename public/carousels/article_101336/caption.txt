POLITICS & BUSINESS:

Trump dismissed Iran's proposal to immediately reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days, calling it unacceptable despite Tehran's insistence that conditions for a deal exist.

Iran's foreign minister stated a reopening could occur if U.S. demands were met, referencing a June MOU that outlined a ceasefire and partial strait access. The waterway, critical for global oil and gas flows, has been largely blocked since February's conflict began, disrupting energy markets. Trump claimed U.S. control over the strait with 'massive amounts of oil' still passing through, though data shows only about 18 daily transits on average.

Iranian officials maintained the MOU remains valid, with President Pezeshkian referencing prior agreements signed in Pakistan. A former National Security Council member noted U.S. skepticism toward reviving the MOU, viewing it as a flawed agreement. Meanwhile, Houthi attacks on Red Sea shipping routes have compounded regional instability, with Saudi Arabia reporting intercepted missile strikes.

Trump hinted at possible military escalation post-November elections, while Iran's envoy stressed that threats of 'annihilation' undermine peace efforts. The U.S. continues its naval blockade of Iranian ports, exacerbating economic strain on Tehran.

The stalemate reflects deeper strategic miscalculations, with both sides clinging to positions that prevent a resolution while global energy markets remain volatile.
