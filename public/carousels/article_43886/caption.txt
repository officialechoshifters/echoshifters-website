ARMY & MILITARY INTERESTS: Nigel Farage faces scrutiny over his ties to Christopher Harborne, a billionaire whose business empire is deeply rooted in the military-industrial complex. Harborne, a major donor to Farage’s Reform UK party, has built a global network of arms-related ventures, including a company once linked to the UK military’s research arm and now developing autonomous weapons. His business dealings also involve U.S. military operations, with a jet fuel supplier that has contracts with the Pentagon.

Harborne’s connections to the armed forces are extensive, including former military officers, pilots, and even a former MI6 agent. He co-founded Subsea Resources, a company involved in retrieving lost aircraft for governments, and later expanded into AML Global, a fuel supplier with a global footprint. AML Global’s clients include the U.S. military, which consumes vast amounts of fuel. The firm’s vice-president, Scott Elder, previously worked for the Pentagon as a host nation liaison in Thailand.

Political analysts warn that Harborne’s influence could shape Farage’s policies, particularly on defense spending. While Farage’s party has not yet aligned with Harborne’s interests, the scale of his donations raises concerns about potential conflicts of interest. Harborne’s legal team insists his commercial ventures and political affiliations are unrelated, but the overlap remains significant.

The case highlights the growing influence of wealthy donors in shaping political agendas. As global tensions rise, the intersection of private enterprise and military power continues to draw attention, with implications for how nations allocate resources and defend their interests.
