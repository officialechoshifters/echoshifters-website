TechCrunch Mobility: The custom chip driving Waymo’s robotaxi ambitions

Waymo's sixth-generation self-driving system is central to its robotaxi ambitions, and the company has now revealed a custom silicon chip designed to handle the massive influx of raw data before it reaches the core “brain” of the self-driving system. The 5 nm ASIC chip delivers more than 1,000 TOPS of computing performance, a statistic that puts it roughly in the same performance range as Nvidia’s latest DRIVE AGX Thor automotive processor. The end result, Waymo says, is a system that has “unmatched efficiency and performance.”

The company listed a slew of partners for the chip, including AMD, Micron, Nvidia, Samsung, Sandisk, Socionext, and TSMC. A little bird told TechCrunch that the Idaho National Laboratory is evaluating whether Chinese lidar sensors might pose a security risk if they become widely used on vehicles in the United States. Several major companies, including Rivian, General Motors, and Ford, said they were unaware of the review. Aurora, Nvidia, and Zoox didn’t respond to questions.

In other news, startup incubated within Rivian has raised another $150 million in a Series D round led by Prysm Capital with participation from existing backers. Serve Robotics, a sidewalk delivery robot company, has partnered with Grubhub to use its sidewalk robots in several cities. Uber has sold its shares in Serve and reduced its use of its robots on the app, set to end next year. Einride, the Swedish electric and autonomous trucking company, has struck a deal with Tesla to buy 500 of its electric Semis.

The latest funding round for startup incubated within Rivian is notable, as is Serve’s ability to diversify its partnerships. Uber’s retreat from Serve illustrates the importance of diversification for companies attempting to scale. With these deals, it’s clear that the companies involved are racing to develop and deploy autonomous vehicles, and that this space is becoming increasingly crowded and competitive.
