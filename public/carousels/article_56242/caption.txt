WORLD STORIES:

Nepal’s rescue teams pulled two survivors from flood-ravaged areas, marking a rare success in the ongoing search for missing workers. A Chinese man was saved from a hydropower tunnel, while a woman was found alive in a collapsed home, both rescued 10 days after catastrophic floods hit the region. The man, who had been trapped for nearly a week, was spotted by rescuers after they detected voices in the tunnel, leading to a joint operation involving Nepalese troops and South Korean experts.

The Trishuli 3A hydropower project remains a focal point, with over 500 workers believed to be stuck in tunnels. Rescue teams from India, China, and the U.S. are working to locate survivors amid extensive damage. The woman, found in a mud-covered home, was given water before being evacuated by helicopter. Officials emphasize that hundreds of workers are still missing, with the search continuing across multiple hydropower sites.

Families of the rescued expressed relief, describing the emotional toll of waiting for news. The man’s brother shared the family’s gratitude, while the woman’s relatives described a 10-day ordeal filled with fear. Rescue efforts are now prioritizing tunnels where workers may still be alive, though conditions remain extremely challenging.

The floods, likely triggered by a glacier collapse, have claimed over 1,200 lives and left more than 5,000 missing. As rescuers push deeper into damaged structures, the focus remains on finding survivors before time runs out. The situation highlights the scale of the disaster and the global collaboration required to navigate such complex rescue operations.
