CRIME STORIES:

A train derailment in northwestern France injured 44 people, with authorities investigating sabotage as a possible cause. The incident occurred Friday evening near Cleon, where a section of rail was found on the tracks. The regional train, carrying 186 passengers from Rouen to Caen, derailed, with one carriage flipping onto its side and four others leaving the rails.

Emergency responders evacuated an 18-year-old woman in critical condition by helicopter to Rouen University Hospital. French prosecutors confirmed the rail fragment could be linked to the incident, though its presence remains unexplained. The investigation is focused on determining whether the derailment was accidental or deliberate, with no conclusive evidence yet.

Local officials described the victims as being in shock, citing shattered windows and flying debris caused by the train’s speed. Cleon Mayor Melanie Delacour reported that some passengers expressed fear of never taking the train again. The derailment has raised concerns about rail safety, particularly given France’s history of major rail accidents.

Authorities have suspended rail traffic on the affected line and deployed over 140 firefighters, five medical teams, and 80 vehicles to the scene. SNCF Reseau, the French rail operator, said services have resumed but will not remove the train until the investigation concludes. The incident highlights the ongoing challenges of ensuring rail safety in France.

The derailment has intensified scrutiny over potential threats to public transportation, especially after past incidents that claimed multiple lives. As the investigation continues, officials emphasize the need for transparency and accountability in addressing rail safety concerns.
