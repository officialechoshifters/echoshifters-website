POLITICS & SPORTS:

At least 68 people have died in a high-speed train collision in Toledo province, Spain, marking the country’s second major rail disaster this year. The accident occurred on the Madrid-Seville line shortly before 9:15 a.m. local time, with one train striking a stopped service at high speed, derailing two carriages and causing significant structural damage.

Transport Minister Óscar Puente stated the control system in Urda failed, halting six trains before one restarted and collided with a stationary train. Regional leader Emiliano García-Page reported 24 fatalities on a Renfe-operated train and the remainder on an Iryo service. Over 500 passengers were aboard both trains, with 50 injured and 58 hospitalized. The crash echoes a January incident in Adamuz that killed 45 people, involving the same rail companies.

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez expressed shock on X, vowing to address safety failures if proven. Opposition leader Santiago Abascal blamed government neglect, calling it a sign of systemic failure. Football’s Copa del Rey postponed all 24 scheduled matches amid the crisis.

The collision followed a January derailment where Iryo’s train hit Renfe’s oncoming service, sending cars down a slope. This week’s crash involved a Renfe train hitting Iryo’s stopped train. Rescue teams continue searching wreckage, with officials noting more victims likely as carriages remain inaccessible.

Spain faces renewed scrutiny over rail safety ahead of a snap election on November 29, where Sánchez’s government must balance crisis management with political fallout from the housing crisis and recent tragedies.
