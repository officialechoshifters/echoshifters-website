ENERGY STORIES:

A new Conservative report claims scrapping UK net-zero policies would save £320bn and cut emissions, but experts say the analysis is flawed. The report argues cheaper electricity would boost EV and heat pump adoption, reducing emissions. However, its alternative scenario predicts higher carbon emissions and slower electrification.

The Onward thinktank’s report, supported by Transira Energy, compares two 2050 pathways. One follows current net-zero policies, while the other abandons the target after 2029. The latter prioritizes cheaper electricity via gas and nuclear, reducing renewables and electrification mandates. Yet, the report’s assumptions about gas prices and nuclear costs are criticized as unrealistic.

Experts warn the model’s conclusions depend on questionable assumptions. For instance, it assumes gas will remain cheap and stable, which is unlikely given current trends. Iain Staffell of Imperial College London calls the report’s modeling “more holes than a Swiss cheese.”

The alternative pathway also leads to slower EV and heat pump adoption due to fewer subsidies and mandates. Critics argue this ignores potential feedback loops, like lower electricity costs increasing electrification. RenewableUK’s Tara Singh highlights the report’s omission of fuel costs for non-electrified transport.

The report’s focus on data centers and AI infrastructure support contrasts with cuts to household electrification grants. Experts say the proposed pathway would increase emissions and delay climate action, contradicting the Conservatives’ claims of a cheaper, greener future.
