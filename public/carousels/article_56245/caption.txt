WORLD STORIES:

U.S. President Donald Trump has dismissed the ongoing conflict with Iran as a 'small potatoes' issue, echoing remarks from his vice president, JD Vance, who claimed the situation shouldn't be called a war. Trump insisted that the U.S. could easily manage the crisis, comparing it to past military operations, while Vance argued that major combat had ceased after about six weeks.

Meanwhile, the U.S. and its allies have escalated diplomatic pressure on Iran, drafting a resolution to refer the country to the U.N. Security Council for alleged noncompliance with nuclear nonproliferation agreements. The resolution, still under negotiation, would allow for potential sanctions or asset freezes, though Russia and China could block such measures due to their veto powers.

Iranian media reported explosions near its key oil hub, Kharg Island, while U.S. officials hinted at possible strikes on suspected nuclear sites, including the enigmatic Pickaxe Mountain, which some analysts believe could be linked to Iran's nuclear program.

The U.S. has also approved a $5 billion arms sale to Saudi Arabia, aimed at enhancing its defensive capabilities amid regional tensions. The sale, pending congressional approval, comes as Iran and its allies continue to target Saudi infrastructure with drone and missile attacks.

As tensions persist, the U.S. and Iran remain locked in a complex standoff, with diplomatic, military, and economic dimensions shaping the broader conflict. The outcome will likely hinge on how international actors navigate the delicate balance of power in the region.
