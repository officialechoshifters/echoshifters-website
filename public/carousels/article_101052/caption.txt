POLITICS:

Sri Lankan courts have handed down life sentences to 15 men for their roles in the 2019 Easter bombings that killed 279 people across churches and luxury hotels. The High Court in Colombo sentenced the defendants to prison terms ranging from 200 to 260 years each, with property confiscation ordered under anti-terrorism laws.

The attacks, carried out on April 21, 2019, targeted three churches and three hotels during Easter celebrations, injuring 574 others. Investigators linked the bombings to a local Islamist extremist group, with all 15 convicted defendants coming from Sri Lanka's minority Muslim community. Prosecutors stated that the group had been radicalized through teachings by cleric Mohamed Ibrahim Mohamed Naufer, who received 240 years.

Nine defendants were acquitted after the court found insufficient evidence. The trial spanned nearly five years, with 23,270 charges filed against 25 suspects in 2021. One suspect died before trial, and seven defendants faced charges without legal representation.

The verdict follows growing scrutiny over intelligence failures prior to the attacks. A parliamentary committee found that officials ignored warnings about the bombings, leading to death sentences for two former security officials who failed to act. The former intelligence chief is currently under investigation for alleged involvement.

This sentencing marks a significant resolution to one of Sri Lanka’s deadliest terror attacks while highlighting systemic intelligence gaps that contributed to the tragedy.
