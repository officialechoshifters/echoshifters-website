POLITICS & HEALTH:

The U.S. Supreme Court has permitted Tennessee to proceed with the execution of Christa Pike, a 50-year-old inmate convicted of murder in 1996, overturning a last-minute stay issued by a federal appeals court.

Pike’s scheduled execution was halted hours before by the Sixth Circuit Court, which needed more time to review her claims of childhood sexual abuse. Tennessee’s attorney general swiftly appealed to the Supreme Court, arguing the stay rewarded procedural delays. Justice Brett Kavanaugh approved the move without explanation.

Three liberal justices dissented, with Justice Sotomayor stating the decision prioritizes state urgency over Pike’s pending legal challenges. Her dissent emphasized the constitutional weight of Pike’s right to life. Pike’s legal team had previously argued Tennessee’s contradiction in denying her abuse claims during sentencing but later acknowledging them during execution proceedings.

Tennessee Governor Bill Lee, a Republican, rejected a clemency request, calling the case a matter of justice for Colleen Slemmer, whose murder Pike committed at age 18. Slemmer’s mother, May Martinez, expressed relief that Pike’s execution would finally occur after decades of waiting.

Pike, diagnosed with bipolar disorder and PTSD, admitted her role in the 1995 killing, which involved torture and dismemberment. Her clemency petition included a heartfelt admission of remorse. This case marks a pivotal moment, as Pike would become the first woman executed in Tennessee in over two centuries.
