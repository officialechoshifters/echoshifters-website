CRIME STORIES:

An ICE officer shot and wounded a man during a traffic stop in Austin, Texas, on Sunday afternoon, officials confirmed. The incident occurred as protests against the Trump administration’s immigration policies erupted near the scene. The victim, identified as Wilber Rafael Garcés Pérez, 28, suffered a torso wound and was hospitalized in stable condition.

Bystander footage captured federal officers in tactical gear standing by as Austin police provided first aid. The officer who fired the weapon remained unresponsive to questions from witnesses. Garcés Pérez, a DoorDash delivery driver from Venezuela, had arrived in the U.S. two years ago. His lawyer reported ICE removed him from a hospital around 4:30 p.m., leaving his family unaware of his whereabouts.

Austin Mayor Kirk Watson called for a third-party investigation, criticizing ICE’s lack of transparency. He emphasized citizens’ right to safety, stating, “This is not the kind of thing that happens in a safe city.” Local demonstrators held signs reading “ICE Nazi” and “Nobody is safe,” while officials confirmed the agent involved has not been located.

The shooting follows a surge in ICE enforcement this summer, including two other fatal incidents in July and August. Critics argue the agency’s aggressive tactics have intensified public fear amid rising immigration arrests exceeding 50,000 monthly for the first time in history.

ICE’s actions have sparked nationwide calls to end its enforcement surges, with advocates warning of systemic failures in oversight and accountability.
