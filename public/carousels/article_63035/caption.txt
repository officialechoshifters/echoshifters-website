CRIME STORIES:

Hong Kong activists who organized annual vigils for the 1989 Tiananmen Square crackdown have been sentenced to prison terms ranging from five to seven years. Lee Cheuk-yan, Chow Hang-tung, and Albert Ho were convicted of inciting subversion under Hong Kong’s national security law. The court ruled they had encouraged others to challenge state authority by promoting the slogan 'ending one-party dictatorship.'

The activists, leaders of the disbanded Hong Kong Alliance, had held large public commemorations for decades, drawing tens of thousands of people before authorities banned such gatherings in 2020. The national security law, imposed by Beijing in 2020, criminalizes dissent and has been criticized for undermining Hong Kong’s autonomy. The trio was charged in 2021 and faced up to 10 years in prison.

Lee and Chow were sentenced to seven years and seven years and three months, respectively, while Ho, who pleaded guilty, received five years. The court also fined the Hong Kong Alliance HK$1.5 million. The sentences have drawn sharp criticism from human rights groups, who called the convictions a 'triple tragedy' for activists, victims, and future generations.

Public reactions ranged from sorrow to defiance. Lee’s wife called the verdict 'unacceptable,' while Chow’s mother urged supporters not to lose heart. Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch condemned the sentences as evidence of a government fearful of historical memory. The cases are seen as a test of Hong Kong’s freedoms under Beijing’s increasing control.
