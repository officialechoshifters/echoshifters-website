POLITICS & BUSINESS:

U.S., Russia, and Ukraine held their first direct peace talks in over nine months in Abu Dhabi, aiming to address Europe’s largest conflict since World War II. The meeting included EU representatives on the sidelines, amid concerns that U.S. pressure might push Kyiv toward territorial concessions favorable to Moscow.

Russian deputy foreign minister Sergei Ryabkov called it too early to assess progress, though talks will continue Wednesday. Ukraine’s president called the discussions 'constructive and substantive,' highlighting talks on prisoner releases and long-term security guarantees. Meanwhile, Russia struck energy infrastructure hours before the talks, causing blackouts in six regions and killing two in Ladyzhyn.

U.S. Secretary Marco Rubio praised the talks as 'productive' but urged European involvement, noting their long-term role in resolving the conflict. European leaders, however, fear Washington may prioritize bilateral deals over a unified NATO-backed framework. EU negotiators warned against U.S. pressure on Ukraine to cede territory.

The talks follow a pattern of escalating Russian attacks on Ukrainian energy sites ahead of diplomatic meetings, as Moscow seeks to heighten pressure before negotiations. European officials reiterated support for a strong NATO-centric security order, though Russian officials framed the talks as part of broader continental security restructuring.

This meeting reflects a pivotal moment where global powers seek to balance immediate ceasefire goals with long-term strategic interests, even as violence continues to escalate on the ground.
