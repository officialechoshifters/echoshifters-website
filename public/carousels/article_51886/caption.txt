HEALTH STORIES:

Search teams have successfully rescued two construction workers from a hydropower tunnel in Nepal, nearly a week after catastrophic floods buried the site. The men, identified as Sanjay Shah and Kabir Maharjan, were pulled alive from the Trishuli 3A project tunnel in Rasuwa district, offering renewed hope for others still trapped in similar locations.

The floods, triggered by a glacier collapse, devastated Nepal and parts of China-controlled Tibet, killing over 1,250 people and displacing tens of thousands. Rescue efforts have been complicated by heavy rainfall and difficult terrain, with hundreds of workers still believed to be missing from hydropower sites. The two survivors were found 170 meters inside the tunnel, where they had been trapped for nine days.

Nepalese officials remain optimistic that more workers could be rescued, citing the presence of air supplies in some tunnels. However, the search has been hindered by challenging weather conditions and the scale of the disaster. Australia has sent a team of drone specialists to assist in the operation, marking the first international aid effort requested by Nepal.

The recovery of the two men highlights the ongoing struggle to locate and save those missing in the aftermath of the floods. With over 5,000 people still unaccounted for, the rescue efforts continue to face significant obstacles, underscoring the severity of the crisis and the need for sustained support.
