ENTERTAINMENT

Ed Sheeran addressed the Israel-Palestine conflict during his first Philadelphia concert since Macklemore's removal as supporting act over pro-Palestinian remarks. He called the October 7 Hamas attack 'horrific' and Israel's Gaza response 'catastrophic and unjustifiable.'

The controversy began when Macklemore was dropped from Sheeran's tour after shouting 'Free Palestine' during an opening act in New Jersey. Multiple supporting artists withdrew in solidarity, including Finneas and Lukas Graham. Sheeran claimed venues threatened to cancel shows if Macklemore remained, denying direct responsibility for his removal.

Pro-Palestinian protesters gathered outside Philadelphia's venue, with one attendee accusing Sheeran of complicity in silencing Macklemore. The Palestinian Campaign for the Academic and Cultural Boycott of Israel urged global tour boycotts, while ticket prices for remaining shows dropped amid spreading calls for cancellation.

Fans remain divided, with some demanding refunds for missing opening acts and others citing personal connections to Sheeran's music. One attendee shared how his wife's ALS diagnosis tied to a song he performed at their wedding, influencing his decision to attend despite the controversy.

Sheeran's stance reflects the challenge of navigating political issues without alienating audiences, as his tour continues amid shifting public sentiment and financial pressures.
