RISKY BUSINESS TECHNOLOGY: Slovakia finds Russian backdoor in traffic speed cameras

Risky Bulletin Newsletter August 19, 2026

Slovakia's national security service NBU has issued a security alert against the use of NERO R-ONE high-speed traffic cameras.

The agency says the cameras contain a backdoor mechanism that grants shell and network access to the devices via an SMS message received from a list of hardcoded Russian phone numbers. The cameras were bought as part of a €30 million EU-funded project to rebuild the country's national traffic monitoring system.

According to the NBU, the cameras are a rebranded version of a Russian traffic camera model named CORDON PRO.M, produced by St. Petersburg-based Russian firm Semicon. The cameras were purchased by the Interior Ministry, which initially denied that the cameras were of Russian origin.

The NBU report also found several security flaws in the cameras, including a turned-off SecureBoot security feature, multiple vulnerabilities in the web management portal, and live streams that can be accessed by anyone without a password.

Interior Ministry officials paused the camera deployment after the NBU report and said it would order an additional assessment from an independent auditor to confirm the findings.

The discovery highlights the potential risks of buying security cameras from Russia, which has been accused of developing malicious backdoors in its technology.

As the situation unfolds, it remains to be seen how Slovakia's government will address this issue and ensure the security of its citizens.

This incident is a reminder that even seemingly secure technology can contain hidden vulnerabilities.

The Slovakian government's decision to pause the camera deployment is a cautious response to this new information.

Only time will tell if this decision will be enough to prevent a larger incident.
