BUSINESS & POLITICS:

Oil prices dipped to $96.90 per barrel Tuesday morning, with Brent benchmarking the decline. The drop follows a $2.48 slide from yesterday’s level and marks a $30 increase from last year’s price. Market shifts reflect ongoing supply-demand dynamics amid global uncertainty.

Crude oil prices influence gas station costs significantly, though pump prices don’t mirror crude movements exactly. Refining, taxes, and station markups shape retail costs, creating a lag when oil prices fall—a phenomenon known as "rockets and feathers." The U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve offers temporary relief during supply shocks, aiming to stabilize energy markets during crises.

Brent crude remains the leading global benchmark, reflecting worldwide oil performance more accurately than WTI. Historical trends show oil prices fluctuate dramatically due to wars, recessions, and policy shifts. For instance, prices plummeted below $20 during the 2020 pandemic and spiked amid 2008 economic turmoil.

Oil’s volatility stems from geopolitical tensions, OPEC decisions, and energy policy changes. Recent shifts include the Trump administration’s push to expand Arctic drilling, reversing Biden-era restrictions. Shale production also plays a role, with increased U.S. access helping moderate price spikes.

Oil’s price swings ripple through the broader economy, affecting everything from grocery costs to shipping expenses. As energy markets remain unpredictable, supply-demand balances and policy decisions will continue to drive market behavior.
