AI & BUSINESS:

Nvidia has launched an independent probe into whether its CEO Jensen Huang was targeted by Chinese intelligence, following a New York Times report linking him to a suspected spy.

The investigation stems from allegations that Huang had a close relationship with Alice Huang, a Singapore-based Megaspeed executive previously suspected of smuggling Nvidia chips to China. U.S. officials believe she acted as a Chinese intelligence asset passing commercial data to Beijing, though Nvidia states no security or national security breaches were detected.

Huang publicly apologized for an extramarital affair, admitting fault and expressing regret for hurting his family. He committed to cooperating with the board's review, emphasizing his dedication to protecting NVIDIA and supporting U.S. national security.

U.S. officials have long restricted Nvidia’s top AI chips to China due to security concerns, with recent export controls aimed at preventing circumvention. Meanwhile, Wall Street saw a sharp tech sell-off, wiping $2 trillion from Nasdaq’s value in a month, with Nvidia’s 3.1% drop contributing significantly.

The probe highlights the growing intersection of corporate leadership, national security, and geopolitical tensions in the AI industry.
